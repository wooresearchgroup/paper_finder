Search tips and citation guidelines

No specific citation format is required

You can search using only a complete article title, DOI, or DOI suffix. For example, d6gc01635a is recognized as an RSC DOI suffix.

For partial citations, enter at least two of the following: author name, article title, journal name or abbreviation, publication year, volume, issue, or page number. Separate the details with spaces, commas, or line breaks.

Common journal abbreviations, including JACS and JOC, are recognized. The search can compare volume and issue numbers, first pages, page ranges, and article numbers. Review the identified fields to make sure they were interpreted correctly. If necessary, add a label such as year: 2024.

The search compares up to 200 candidate records and displays up to 25 results. If one paper can be identified unambiguously from the supplied details, only that result is displayed. You can also paste references in ACS, APA, or Vancouver style, as well as BibTeX or RIS entries.

How citation details are recognized
Paper title
Full and partial titles are compared with indexed article titles. Quotation marks or a title: label help preserve chemical locants and numbers during parsing.
DOI and DOI suffix
Full DOIs and DOI URLs are looked up directly. Recognized suffix patterns, including those used by RSC and ACS, are used to reconstruct candidate DOIs, which are then checked against registered metadata. DOI fragments of at least six characters are checked against DOI strings; a partial match is not considered definitive.
Journal names and abbreviations
A chemistry-focused lookup table recognizes journal names and common abbreviations, including JACS and JOC. Variations in capitalization, periods, and spacing are accepted. If a journal is not recognized, use a journal: label.
Year, volume, and issue
The parser recognizes labels such as year:, vol:, and issue:, as well as volume(issue) notation and common citation formats. An unlabeled four-digit number may be interpreted as a publication year, depending on context.
Page ranges and article numbers
The parser recognizes first-page numbers, full or abbreviated page ranges, and article numbers beginning with e. It checks page ranges before identifying standalone publication years to reduce ambiguity.
Ambiguous input
Inferred fields are labeled, and unclassified numbers are checked against the bibliographic details of each candidate. If multiple papers have similar citation details, additional information may be needed.

Search queries are sent to Crossref. This page does not save your search history. Full-text access depends on the publisher and your institution’s subscriptions.
